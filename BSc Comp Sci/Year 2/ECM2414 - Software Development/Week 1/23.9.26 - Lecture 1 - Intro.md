#### Software Development Lifecycle (SDLC)
The point of the SDLC is to formalise the process of developing software and to improve the quality of the produced software.

**Requirement Analysis** - Understand the client's problem and formalise a set of requirements that the software must achieve. You also determine if the problem can be achieved using a computer.

**Design** - Set out the hardware, software, and network requirements and determine which parts of the system will be made in-house or what will be externally contracted.

**Implementation** - Write the software based on the design.

**Testing** - Test the software to see if it meets all the requirements set out in the *Requirements Analysis* stage.

**Evolution** - Test the system with the stakeholders and improve the system based on their feedback.


#### SDLC Methodologies
**Waterfall** - Breaks the SDLC into distinct phases such that a phases can only start once the previous phases is fully completed and signed off. This means that any errors not resolved in the correct stage will propagate through the other phases and are expensive to fix.

**Prototyping** - Quickly prototype a solution to gain immediate feedback from the stakeholders; often leads to reanalysing the initial requirements. 

**Rapid application Development** - A heavy focus on development tools to reduce the development time and cost. The deadline is prioritised over the requirements, leading to a reduction in requirements to meet the deadline.

**Agile** - Development based around short iterative *sprints*, where a system is produced, feedback is given, then another, better system is built. This produces a *minimum viable product* - a product with the bare minimum number of features to showcase them to the stakeholders.

A *backlog* is a list of features that must be implemented in the near future.


#### Scrum
Short fixed length sprints - typically 2-4 weeks long
Aims to produce deployable software in each sprint


#### Pair Programming
One is called the **driver** - the person who writes the code

The other is called the **navigator** - the person who is responsible for the overall direction of the programming

Roles are switched periodically and provides better productivity than two developers working independently.


#### How to choose a methodology
This depends on the project type; yours, your employer's, and your customer's preferences; and yours and your teams abilities.

However, you may choose to combine different traits from different methodologies.
