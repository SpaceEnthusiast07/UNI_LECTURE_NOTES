
#### Specialisation
**Specialisation** is the process of *splitting up an entity into sub entities* based on *distinguishing characteristics* of those entities.

Specialisation is a *top-down* design!

The idea of specialisation is almost identical to the idea of inheritance with OOP, specifically the relationship between parent and child classes.

A perfect example is with the `Staff` entity, which can be split up into sub-entities based on the `Staff`'s role: `Manager`, `Sales`, `HR`, `Secetary`, etc. Below is the ER diagram for this example:

![[ERD_Specialisation_Example 2.svg|285]]

You might wonder what `{Optional,AND}` means; well, for a specialisation or generalisation (see below) relationship, you must specify the *participation of the super entity* and what the *disjoint constraint for the sub entities* is. 
 - For participation
	 - *Optional* means that an entity in the super entity type doesn't have to be one of the defined sub entity types, it could be another.
	 - *Mandatory* means that an entity of the super entity type must be one of the defined sub entity types.
 - For the disjoint constraint
	 - *AND (non-disjoint)* means there *is overlap between the sub entity types*; i.e., an entity of the super entity type can be multiple of the sub entity type.
	 - *OR (disjoint)* means there is no overlap between the sub entity types; i.e., an entity of the super entity type can only be one sub entity.

To achieve specialisation, you first start with the super entity, `Staff` in the above example, then you determine the sub entities based on some common characteristic between the entities. In the above example, the common characteristic is the `Staff`'s role.


#### Generalisation
**Generalisation** is about *combining* multiple *closely related entities into one super entity*.

You may notice that the definition of generalisation is exactly the reverse of that of specialisation, that is because it is! Generalisation is a *bottom-up* design.
