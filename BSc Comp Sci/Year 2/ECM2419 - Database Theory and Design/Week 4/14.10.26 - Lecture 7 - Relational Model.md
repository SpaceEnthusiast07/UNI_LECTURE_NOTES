#### Relational Model
A **relation** is a *table that represents an entity* from an entity-relationship diagram that *must adhere to certain properties*; hence, some tables are not relations.
****
An **attribute** is exactly the same as with an ER diagram; a *property* of an entity or relation, such as a students name or age.

A **tuple** is a *row in the relation* (table); also called a record.

The **degree** of a relation is the *number of attributes* it has.

Then, the **cardinality** of a relation is the *number of tuples* or rows it has.

#### Relational Keys
These are the same idea as with keys in the entity-relationship model.

A **candidate key** is the *set of all attributes* that can be *used to uniquely identify each tuple* (record) in the relation.

The **primary key** is the *chosen attribute from the set of candidate keys*.

The **alternate keys** are all other candidate keys that were not chosen to be the primary key.

The **foreign key** is the *primary key from another relation or entity* that is used to mark the relationship between the two relations/entities. They are the relational model's form of the relationship lines in an entity-relationship diagram; hence, the ER model doesn't contain any foreign keys.


#### Relational Scheme vs Instance
A **relational schema** is the *definition* only of a relation including its name and attributes. Below is an example of a relational schema:
```Relational_Schema

Primary Key: stdID
Alternate Key: stdEmail
```
