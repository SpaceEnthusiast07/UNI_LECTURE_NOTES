
#### Solving a Problem
There are four steps:

**Understand the Problem**
You need to *carefully study* the problem to understand it. Although this may seem obvious, *failing to do so* will defiantly *result in a failure* to solve it.
Here are some questions to ask yourself:
 - What are you being asked to find/show?
 - Can you *restate* the question in your own words?
 - Can you think of a *picture/diagram* that might help?
 - Do you understand all the words in the question?

**Devise a Plan**
This involves identifying the calculations, computations, or constructions that must be performed in order to solve the problem.
Some key questions are:
 - Did you use all the data given?
 - Did you use the whole condition?
 - Could you *use a related problem* that has already been solved?

**Carry out the Plan**
All this involves is carrying out the planned steps and all that should be needed is care and patience.
Questions to ensure you have executed your plan correctly:
 - Can you see each step is correct?
 - Can you prove each step is correct?

**Look back and reflect**
This involves taking time to consider what happened correctly and what didn't. These observations can make future problems easier to solve.
Questions to ask yourself:
 - Can you check the result?
 - Can you check the argument?
 - Can you use the result/method for some other problem?
