
#### Threat Modelling
Securing a system is expensive and time consuming, but not all systems or parts of a system are rewarding to an attacker. Therefore, we use a resource allocation approach that involves risk-based cost-benefit analysis. You label systems or parts of systems as high value, low risk, etc.

**Threat modelling** involves *analysing a representation of a system*, such as a UML Object Diagram, to *highlight concerns about security* and privacy characteristics. The result of this process is a document containing threats alongside their mitigations.

You may think this sounds obvious; well, it is, and actually you perform threat modelling every single day. For example, when you cross the road, you analyse the risk of crossing the road by looking for cars coming from the left or the right.

**What are you protecting?**
How valuable an asset is will determine how much effort will be invested in protecting it.

**Against whom?**
Is the attacker casually attacking or targeting your system. A targeted attacker is more motivated and likely to gain entry.

**What are the most vulnerable points?**
Higher valued assets are naturally going to be protected more; hence, time and effort will be invested more in them.

**What are some counter measures?**
You can choose whether to employ more expensive and extensive security measures, or cheap, easy to implement ones.

There is a fine balance between security and usability. By adding too much security to a system, you can hinder its usability and effectively force users away.

To analyse the security of the technical architecture, you can use STRIDE; for attacker goals and attack paths, you can use Attack Trees, which sounds intuitive; for Risk and business impact, use PASTA; and finally, for privacy and data protection, use LINDDUN. However, in this module we are only concerned with STRIDE.


#### Threat Modelling Process
This process is method-agnostic, meaning it doesn't rely on certain threat modelling approaches; it is appropriate for all.

**Scope and Assets**
Build a of representation of the system, e.g. using UML, and identify assets, security objectives, and trust boundaries.

**Identify Threads**
Use a threat modelling approach, such as STRIDE, to identify threats in the representation of the system.

**Assess Risk and Plan Mitigation**
Prioritise each threat by its likelihood (chance attacker will attempt to exploit threat) and impact (how the exploited threat will affect the company in different ways). You need to perform risk treatment: accept and forget; eliminate completely; mitigate or now; or transfer responsibility to another party.

**Validate and Iterate**
Verify the risk treatment has worked and security controls are correct, and update as the design changes.
