
#### Mandatory Access Control (MAC)
Unlike *RBAC*, which assigns "roles" to subjects (users) and allows certain "roles" to access certain objects (documents), **MAC** works from the other end by giving individual objects (documents) **security labels**, such as "Unclassified", "Confidential", "Secret" and "Top Secret", to control access to them.

Then, each subject (person) is given a **formal authorisation** (security clearance) for objects (documents), corresponding to the "security labels" for the documents. Each subject (person) can also access all objects (documents) below their security clearance.


#### MAC in a Historical Context
The information on paper is stored in *opaque folders* that have their security label *clearly displayed* on the front.

Each subject (user) is cleared to *see (read, not write)* objects (documents) up to their security clearance level.

All documents are *physically secured* and subjects (users) must show a *guard* their clearance level before they are allowed to take a folder/document. Obviously, if these security measures are violated, it is a serious offense and often *considered treason*.
